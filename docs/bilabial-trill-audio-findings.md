# Bilabially Post-Trilled Dental Stops: Audio and Video Review

## Scope

Four videos were reviewed from the source folder:

- `Tpi tir.mp4`
- `Tpır tırs.mp4`
- `dı dbı.mp4`
- `tı tpı.mp4`

Audio was extracted to WAV, frame strips were extracted for visual review, and waveform/spectrogram plots were generated for each clip.

## Findings

The set supports a plausible phonological contrast between plain aspirated dental stops and bilabially post-trilled dental stops, but the strength of the evidence varies by clip.

- `Tpi tir.mp4`: looks like a plain aspirated dental control. The waveform shows two main speech events with a simple stop-release pattern, and the frame strip does not show repeated bilabial closures.
- `Tpır tırs.mp4`: looks like the clearest bilabially post-trilled target. The visual frames show repeated lip constriction/closure behavior that is not present in the plain control, and the audio looks more complex than a simple aspirated stop release.
- `dı dbı.mp4`: likely contains the bilabially post-trilled member of the contrast, but this is the weakest video case because only three frames were available from extraction. The audio still looks more compact and less like a plain aspirated control than `tı tpı.mp4`.
- `tı tpı.mp4`: looks like a plain aspirated dental control. The audio has one main release with aspiration-like energy and no convincing repeated bilabial closure pattern in the frames.

## Conclusion

Overall, the four videos are consistent with a real contrast between a plain aspirated dental stop and a bilabially post-trilled dental stop. The strongest evidence is visual in `Tpır tırs.mp4` and `tı tpı.mp4`, with `dı dbı.mp4` remaining lower-confidence because of sparse extracted frames.
