# Video / Lip-Motion Review: Bilabially Post-Trilled Dental Stops

## Scope

Four close-up videos were inspected:

- `Tpi tir.mp4`
- `Tpır tırs.mp4`
- `dı dbı.mp4`
- `tı tpı.mp4`

Representative full-face contact sheets and mouth-focused frame strips were extracted in the `video-analysis` subtree.

## Findings

- `Tpır tırs.mp4` shows the clearest repeated lip constriction / closure behavior. The mouth strip shows multiple cycles of narrowing, near-closure, reopening, and renewed constriction in the region of the target consonant, which is not what the plain controls look like.
- `Tpi tir.mp4` looks like a plain aspirated dental control. The mouth motion is comparatively smooth and does not show the same repeated bilabial contact pattern.
- `tı tpı.mp4` also looks closer to a plain dental control than to the bilabially post-trilled target. There is ordinary speech-related lip movement, but no convincing repeated lip-closure sequence.
- `dı dbı.mp4` is the weakest visual case because the usable lip-motion window is less clear, but it still does not look like a plain aspirated control. The visual evidence is compatible with the same contrast, though with lower confidence than `Tpır tırs.mp4`.

## Conclusion

The video evidence supports a real visual contrast between the plain aspirated dental stops and the bilabially post-trilled dental stops. The strongest positive evidence is in `Tpır tırs.mp4`, and the plain controls do not show the same repeated bilabial closure pattern.

The contrast is visually clearest in the `Tpır tırs` vs. `Tpi tir` / `tı tpı` comparison; `dı dbı` remains the weakest and least cleanly extracted case.
